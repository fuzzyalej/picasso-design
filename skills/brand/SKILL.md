# Brand

Brand content must be specific enough to act on. "Clean and modern" means nothing; "Helvetica-weight, tight kerning, no decorative elements" means something. Prefer real references (brands, fonts, colors) over adjectives, and give every decision a one-sentence rationale.

## Foundation
Mission and Vision, one sentence each. Values: 4 to 5, each one sentence. Personality: about five words. Positioning: one sentence.

## Verbal identity
- Voice Essence: one fill-in sentence, "[Brand] sounds like [character]: [quality], [quality], [quality]."
- Tone axes: 4 to 5 (Formal to Casual, Calm to Energetic, Serious to Witty, Accessible to Authoritative, Distant to Intimate). Tone flexes per channel; voice stays constant.
- Voice qualities: 5 to 6, each with a Do and a Don't.
- Vocabulary: words we use, words we avoid, proprietary terms.
- Writing style: sentence length, punctuation, capitalization, numbers, contractions.
- Things We Never Say: an explicit list.

## Visual identity
Logo direction, clear space, minimum size, incorrect-usage rules. Color table (name, value, role). Type hierarchy. Imagery and iconography direction. Three to four design principles.

## Brand Don'ts
Close with a consolidated checklist pulled from every section.

## Discipline
Never invent facts, metrics, or history for effect. Run the `unslop-copy` skill on all prose. No em-dashes.
